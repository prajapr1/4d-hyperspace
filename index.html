How 4D Visualization in 3D Works

Human beings perceive physical reality in 3 spatial dimensions: $(x, y, z)$. A 4-dimensional object has an additional fourth spatial axis $w$, forming points $(w, x, y, z)$ perpendicular to width, height, and depth.

1. The Four 3D Coordinate Subspaces

Just as a 3D object casts 2D shadows onto $(x,y)$, $(y,z)$, and $(x,z)$, a 4D object projects into four primary 3D coordinate subspaces:

  • (w, x, y) Subspace: Orthogonal to $Z$. Slicing at $z = z_0$ isolates this cross-section.
  • (x, y, z) Subspace: The familiar 3D world! Orthogonal to $W$. Slicing at $w = w_0$ gives the exact 3D cross-section at that 4th-dimensional slice.
  • (y, z, w) Subspace: Orthogonal to $X$.
  • (w, x, z) Subspace: Orthogonal to $Y$.

2. Dimensional Reduction via Slicing

  • A 0D Point in 4D intersects a 3D hyperplane in at most one 0D Point.
  • A 1D Line intersects in at most a 0D Point (yellow glowing bead).
  • A 2D Plane intersects in a 1D Line segment (illuminated laser line).
  • A 3D Volume intersects in a 2D Polygon cross-section.
  • A 4D Hypervolume (Tesseract, Hypersphere) intersects in a complete 3D Solid!

3. 4D Rotations (6 Planes)

In 4D space, rotation occurs around a plane (not a line axis!). The 6 orthogonal planes are $XW, ZW, YW, XY, XZ, YZ$. Rotations in planes involving $W$ cause parts of the object to traverse the 4th dimension, causing their 3D shadows to fold, turn inside-out, and re-emerge!

4. Focus Mode & Keyboard Shortcuts

  • F: Toggle Focus / Fullscreen Mode (large view + 3 sideways boxes)
  • Esc: Return to 2x2 Grid View
  • Spacebar: Toggle 4D Auto-Rotation
  • S: Toggle Camera Synchronization
  • Click any side box in Focus Mode: Promotes it to the main viewport!
